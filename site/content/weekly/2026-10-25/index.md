## Commentary

Day-ahead power averaged 19.85 c/kWh in the week to 25 October, 8.5% cheaper than the previous week's 21.70 but still among the dearest weeks of the last three months. Daily averages ran from 9.06 on Saturday 24 Oct to 27.63 on Monday 19 Oct.

**Two weeks in one.** Monday to Friday averaged between 21.40 and 27.63 c/kWh. Saturday and Sunday came in at 9.06 and 9.07. The two dearest days were the two lowest-wind days: Monday, when wind covered barely a tenth of demand, and Thursday, when it covered about a quarter. Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday had wind covering roughly half of demand and still averaged 21.40 to 23.11.

The weekend does not fit a wind story on its own. Wind covered more than half of demand on both [Saturday](https://www.inisenergy.com/insights/daily/2026/10/24/) and [Sunday](https://www.inisenergy.com/insights/daily/2026/10/25/), and demand was lower, with Sunday the lowest of the week. But Friday had a similar wind share and averaged 21.40. Lower demand did some of the work. The shape of the day tells you more than the average. Sunday's peak was higher than Friday's, so this is not about the evening. It is about the floor. Friday never got cheap: its cheapest half-hour was dearer than either weekend day's average. Sunday's cheapest half-hour was priced at zero. Why the floor fell away is not in the dailies, so I will not guess.

Sunday was the long trading day: the clocks went back, so it ran an hour over and the week has one extra hour of prices, as the period count above shows.

**Where that leaves the week.** 3rd dearest of the last 14 weeks; last dearer: week of 12 Oct. Against the previous 13 weeks: dearer. In plain terms: expensive against the last three months, and the dearest of the five weeks compared against the same time last year.

**Renewing soon.** One cheap, windy weekend does not make a fixed price for a year or two cheap. Suppliers price fixed contracts off forward costs, not last Sunday. What a week this volatile does tell you is that quotes can move sharply in a few days. Collect them over a short window, and do not sit on one waiting for a better week. A [free bill comparison](/compare) is the quickest way to put several side by side.

**Out of contract or on rollover.** You are on a rate the supplier set, not one you negotiated, and wholesale is only one part of your unit rate, alongside network charges, the PSO levy and supplier margin, among other things. Weekdays like this week's are why this is the riskiest place to be. Get a quote. Here is [what happens on rollover](/blog/business-energy-rollover-contracts) if you are not sure whether that is you.

**Fixed for the long haul.** Your unit rate does not change week to week. The one job is to diary your contract end date and the notice window, because missing that is how people end up on rollover. How [rollover, notice windows and contract end dates work for SMEs](/solutions/sme) is worth a read before you do.

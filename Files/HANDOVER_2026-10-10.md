## Part A: The plain-English version

**What this is.** A blog that publishes Irish electricity market content for SME owners. Eoin is a broker (INIS Energy). The blog exists to make him look like the broker who reads the market honestly. It originally published a daily briefing. The direction is now a **weekly "Was this week normal?" post**, with the daily briefing demoted to a data feed.

**What happened since 5 October.** The weekly format went from idea to a working pipeline, and a lot of plumbing underneath it was rebuilt:

- The store (`data/history.db`) caught up and now holds **a year of prices** (7 Oct 2025 to 9 Oct 2026) and 5 months of wind and demand.
- Every join between prices and wind/demand was found to be 24 hours misaligned for two periods a day (periods 1-2, 23:00-00:00 the evening before). That is fixed. Roughly 140 already-published daily posts carry slightly wrong demand or wind-% figures. They are baselined in the audit, not edited.
- The pipeline now handles clock-change days (50 and 46 periods) by deriving the trading day from `zoneinfo`, not from a fixed UTC offset.
- `weekly_summary()` computes the weekly verdict from the store, with exact definitions, suppression rules and independent SQL verification.
- `weekly.py` builds a draft post and chart from the store, with no network calls.
- The audit checks weekly posts exactly, including numbers typed into the commentary.
- **The first weekly post, 28 Sep to 4 Oct, is published** (commit da1a1e9).
- **Cron is installed (13:20, 10 Oct).**

**Where it stands.** The pipeline is sound. Remaining risk is operational: the first unattended Monday (12 Oct) and the clock change (25 Oct).

**The strategic idea, unchanged.** Depth in time beats width in feeds. ### B3. Data pipeline

**Feed 1: SEMOpx DAM.** `reports.semopx.com`, EA-001 "SEM-DA PWR-MRC-D+1". Semicolon-delimited, European decimal commas, wide/transposed, key block `Index prices;30;EUR`. Timestamps UTC. Only SEM-DA valid.
- **The trading day runs 23:00 to 23:00 Irish time.** It has 48 periods normally, **50 on the autumn change day (26 Oct 2025 verified; 25 Oct 2026 upcoming), 46 on the spring one (29 Mar 2026 verified)**. The old "22:00Z-21:30Z" is a summer fact. Verified from real files.
- **Publish lag:** the DA file for delivery day D appears at about 00:00-01:00 on **D+1**. It never exists on D itself. `run_daily`'s default delivery date is now **yesterday**.
- **Retention is a rolling ~1 year.** 2025-10-06 is proven gone; 2025-10-07 onward was fetched. The window moves forward daily, so earlier history cannot be re-fetched. **The store is the only copy of last year's prices.**

**Feed 2: EirGrid Smart Grid Dashboard.** `smartgriddashboard.com/api/chart/`, region ROI. Fields `WIND_ACTUAL`, `WIND_FCAST`, `SYSTEM_DEMAND`. 15-min resampled to 30-min. Timestamps are **local, not UTC** (the long day repeats 01:00-01:45 in the forecast; wind actual and demand list that hour once, so 4 half-hours on 25 Oct will stay NULL: honest, and only affects that one post).
- **The demand endpoint is flaky:** it returns `{"Rows":[]}` with HTTP 200, intermittently, flipping within minutes per date. Retries within seconds don't help. Wind is unaffected. The fix is `store.py --heal`, a separate later pass. Wind is always stored; demand and wind_pct are NULL (never 0) when demand is empty.
- No demand forecast exists. Absent from the data: interconnectors, fuel mix beyond wind, solar, SNSP, imbalance, curtailment, gas, forwards. "Curtailment" in old commentary is inference.

### B5. The weekly method (`weekly_stats.weekly_summary`)

- A week is Monday-Sunday of delivery dates. It **raises** if any day is missing or short of its expected period count.
- **Trailing baseline:** the previous 13 complete weeks, counting only complete ones (it does not skip back over gaps). **Seasonal baseline:** weeks starting 364 days earlier ±14 days (5 weeks). Both exclude the target week and everything later, so regenerated output is reproducible (verified non-vacuously).
- **Suppression:** verdict and rank suppressed below n=8 (trailing) or n=4 (seasonal), with a plain-words reason in the post.
- **Percentile method:** strictly-below share plus half the ties. **Verdict:** 20th-80th percentile = "normal"; outside = "dearer/cheaper"; beyond 5th/95th = "unusually". Volatility uses `median_arb_spread` and says "more volatile"/"calmer".
- Fields: week mean (€/MWh and c/kWh = ÷10), daily means, rank (1 = dearest) and "dearest/cheapest since", periods above €150/€200 with trailing medians, median arb spread and rank, `wind_mw_mean` vs trailing median with `wind_coverage`. A 50-period day counts 50 times in the week mean.
- **Weekly posts use wind in MW only, never wind %**, and never mention gas, interconnectors, outages, forward curves or arbitrage.
- **Seasonal timeline:** 28 Sep week has 1 seasonal week, 12 Oct has 3, **19-25 Oct (post on Mon 26 Oct) is the first with 4**. The seasonal marker on the chart has only been rendered from synthetic data.
- Independent SQL recompute matched on 5 weeks. All tests pass (B8).

**First published result (28 Sep - 4 Oct 2026):** week mean €184.17/MWh (18.42 c/kWh); **rank 4 of 14**; dearest since 21 Sep, cheapest since 14 Sep; verdict "normal" (rank 4 of 14 is about the 77th percentile, 3 points under the 80th, so one rank step flips the label); volatility "normal" (median daily spread €130.13/MWh); 280 of 336 periods above €150 (trailing median 232); 102 above €200 (median 72); wind 1743.1 MW vs 865.6 MW trailing median. Daily means (€/MWh): Mon 209.05, Tue 158.37, Wed 142.52, Thu 188.95, Fri 196.56, Sat 215.83, Sun 177.92. **Takeaway:** wind moved the days but not the level; "normal" is relative to a mostly-summer baseline in a rising market.

### B6. Operations and scheduling

- **`run_daily.py`**: default delivery date is yesterday. `--store-only` catches up the store from `MAX(market_prices.date)+1` through yesterday (max 14 dates, oldest first, 1s apart, via `persist_day`), then heals NULL-demand dates in the last 14 days. Exit 0 only if the sole gap is yesterday's price not yet published; nonzero otherwise.
- **Loud failure:** a store write failure now prints `STORE NOT WRITABLE` / `STORE WRITE FAILED <date>: <exc>` and exits nonzero after the post is generated. (Before, it was swallowed. Probable cause of the five frozen store days, 5-9 Oct: `chmod 444` on the DB I told Eoin to set. Not proven by a log line.)
- **Overwrite guard:** `scaffold_daily` raises `PostExistsError` unless the existing post is `draft: true` with a body identical to the scaffold output. `--force` is the only way through. **Do not use `run_daily --date` to catch up; it regenerates posts that now hold commentary.**
- **Stored-day skip:** if the store already holds the full day, `run_daily` skips the persist step and doesn't need a writable DB.
- **Archive protection** (`14cb8a8`): a response with no rows never replaces an archive that has rows, in any mode.
- **Rebuild safety** (`b298bff`): `--rebuild-conditions` skips a date (and keeps stored values) when the archive, or the previous day's archive for periods 1-2, is empty.
- **`scripts/nightly.sh`**: `flock` (exit 75 if overlapping), one dated backup per day in `~/backups` keeping the newest 14 (other backups untouched), `chmod 644` before and `444` after via a trap (even on SIGTERM), runs `venv/bin/python pipeline/run_daily.py --store-only`, logs to `logs/nightly-YYYY-MM.log`. DB path overridable by env var for tests. A manual run under `env -i` at 13:16 on 10 Oct exited 0, left the DB at mode 444 with its mtime unchanged, and logged "store is current through 2026-10-09".
- **Cron: INSTALLED 13:20, 10 Oct** (`crontab /tmp/inis.cron`; `crontab -l` shows these four lines):

  The 10:00 Monday line is the retry for the 07:30 build. Cron lines must go in through a quoted heredoc (`<<'EOF'`) or `crontab -e`, never pasted into the shell (`*` expands to filenames and every line fails on its first word).

### B9. Lessons that must carry over

1. **Ground truth first.** Compare artifacts to a fresh computation from source, never representation against representation. The wind check on the weekly post (mean of 7 daily wind figures = 1743 MW) was done this way.
2. **Triage on the right axis.** Test structural validity as a boolean, not by magnitude.
3. **The silent-failure class.** Things that look right and do nothing: `WIND_FCAST` vs `WIND_FORECAST`, `.Rows[]` vs `.[]`, `+1` vs `+4` slice, clock-string comparison across 23:00, **`db_path=DB_PATH` default arguments bind at definition time** (a test wrote 96 rows into the real DB), **`row.get("Value") or row.get("value")` drops a real 0**, **an empty-file archive counted as "existing"**, **a placeholder detector that matches one string while the empty-block failure is a different one**, **a swallowed `try/except` around the store write plus a `chmod 444`**. If something "works but returns nothing", suspect this first.
4. **Narration is unreliable.** Claude Code has reported "tested" for untested commits, "done" for the opposite of what was asked, and a guessed URL as a finding. Verify with `git log`, `git diff`, `grep`, SQL.
5. **Fix at the right layer.** Flaky feeds need a later heal pass, not retries. A wrong join is fixed in the join, with the old posts baselined, not hand-edited.
6. **Baseline, don't widen.** Widening an audit tolerance to match a wrong post teaches the audit to agree with errors.
7. **Protect evidence before backfilling.** Raw archives and the store are not in git. Back up first, and never let an empty response overwrite a good one.
8. **A guard that blocks the only legitimate writer is a bug.** `chmod 444` plus nothing to lift it is how the store froze for five days.
9. **Hand-copied derived numbers with no link to source** are the root cause of the original 112-post bug. Commentary is where it returns, hence the commentary-number audit.
10. **Cheap spot day is not a good time to fix.**
11. **Pasting multi-line config into a shell runs it.** Use a quoted heredoc.
